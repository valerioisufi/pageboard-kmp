# Antialiased ink

The strokes drawn with antialiased edges, like the rest of the page, on every platform: in the
tiles, in the stroke being drawn, in the selection moved, in the thumbnails. This document starts
from the stair-stepped ink seen on a Windows desktop at 100% (see
[Tile sharpness](tile-sharpness.md), B3) (2026-10-16).

The responsibilities are assigned following the GRASP principles (Information Expert, Creator,
Low Coupling, Protected Variations).

## Starting point

A stroke is modeled by Google Ink (`InkStrokeEngine`, through the native bridge) into a triangle
mesh (`StrokeMesh`: positions and indices), and `StrokeMeshPainter` draws the triangles with
`drawVertices`. Neither Skia nor Android antialias `drawVertices`: the edges of the ink are
stair-stepped. On a screen of 2x and more the steps are half a pixel and are hardly seen, on a
screen of 1x they are; shrunk in the thumbnails, a thin stroke breaks into dashes.

The strokes are stored as their inputs (`StrokeData`) and modeled again at every load
(`StrokeData.toElement` with `InkStrokeModeller`). The PDF export models every stroke once more
to get its outline (`InkStrokeEngine.buildOutline`): the closed polygons whose union, filled with
the non-zero winding rule, is the shape of the stroke (`StrokeOutline`).

### How Google Ink draws on Android

`CanvasStrokeRenderer` of `androidx.ink` has two renderers:

* **Mesh** (Android 14 and later): `android.graphics.Mesh` with shaders generated by the native
  library. Every vertex carries, besides its position, its distance from the edges of the stroke
  (side and forward derivatives and labels); the vertex shader moves the vertices of the edges
  outward by a fraction of a pixel, the fragment shader computes the coverage of every pixel from
  its distance to the edges.
* **Path** (before Android 14): the outlines of the stroke filled as a `Path` with an
  antialiased paint and the non-zero rule, cached per mesh, rebuilt for the stroke in progress
  when it changes.

The mesh renderer cannot be repeated here: Skiko (desktop, iOS) has no binding of `SkMesh`, the
tiles are rasterized on the CPU, and our minimum Android version is 29. The path renderer can: the
bridge already gives the outlines (`ink_stroke_builder_get_outlines`, from
`InProgressStroke::GetCoatOutlines`, available while the stroke is drawn too).

### Measures

A probe on the desktop (JVM, native Ink): 300 strokes of handwriting of 60 to 150 inputs, pen of
0.5 mm.

| | Triangles | Outlines as paths |
|---|---|---|
| Modeling the 300 strokes | 578 ms | 580 ms |
| Memory of the geometry | 1258 KB (positions and indices) | 721 KB more |
| A tile of 512 px with the 300 strokes (4.19 px/mm) | 2.35 ms | 3.51 ms |

* Modeling a stroke takes about 1.9 ms: the outline must come from the modeling that makes the
  mesh, not from a second one (as the export does now).
* Every vertex of the mesh is on the outline, one polygon per stroke: the outline is as many
  floats as the positions.

## Requirements

1. The ink is antialiased wherever it is drawn: tiles, strokes baked at once, the stroke being
   drawn, the selection, the strokes of the others, the thumbnails, the images exported.
2. A translucent stroke (highlighter) is painted once where it overlaps itself, as now.
3. The stored data does not change: the outline is computed with the mesh at every load.
4. A stroke is modeled once: the outline comes from the same modeling as the mesh.
5. Without the native engine (no outline) the strokes are drawn as now.

## Design

* **Information Expert.** The modeller knows the shape of the stroke, so `StrokeMesh` carries it:
  `outline: StrokeOutline?`, given by the native modeller (`NativeStrokeEngineDelegate.currentMesh`
  for the stroke in progress, the finished one and the one loaded), `null` from the Kotlin
  modeller. `withPoints` keeps it, `transformed` maps it with the vertices: a stroke moved, scaled
  or turned keeps the outline of its mesh.
* **Low Coupling.** The painters read the outline from the mesh: nothing else changes in the tiles,
  the overlays or the export, which draw a `StrokeMesh` already.
* **Protected Variations.** `StrokeMeshPainter` (expect/actual) chooses how to draw: the outline
  filled as an antialiased path (Skia `PathBuilder.addPoly` on Skiko, `android.graphics.Path` on
  Android), the triangles when there is no outline. The non-zero rule paints a translucent stroke
  once where it overlaps itself, so the outline needs no offscreen layer.
* **Creator.** The export takes the outline of the stroke (`stroke.mesh.outline`) instead of
  modeling it again: `InkStrokeEngine.buildOutline` goes.

## Plan

|---|---|---|
| 1 | The outline with the mesh: `StrokeMesh.outline` (kept by `withPoints`, mapped by `transformed`), filled by the native modeller; the export uses it (`buildOutline` removed); tests; ABI of `drawview-model` and `drawview-ink` | Done |
| 2 | The painters fill the outline: `StrokeMeshPainter` on Skiko and Android, the triangles as fallback; tests on the pixels (antialiased edges, translucent overlaps painted once); PencilNotes on the screen of 1x | Done |

### Phase 1 results

* `StrokeMesh.outline`: the native modeller gives it with every mesh (the stroke in progress, the
  finished one, the one loaded), through the vertices of the mesh; `withPoints` keeps it,
  `transformed` maps it (`InkStrokeEngineTest`). The Kotlin modeller gives none
  (`KotlinStrokeEngineTest`).
* The PDF export fills the outline of the mesh of the stroke instead of modeling the stroke once
  more (`PdfExporterTest` unchanged).
* ABI: `StrokeMesh` has the outline in its constructor and in `fromIndexedMesh`;
  `InkStrokeEngine.buildOutline` is removed. The dumps of the JVM and of Android are generated;
  the klib dumps are edited by hand on Windows (where the iOS targets cannot be dumped) and to be
  checked on macOS.

### Phase 2 results

* `StrokeMeshPainter` fills the outline with an antialiased paint and the non-zero rule: Skia
  `PathBuilder` (one `addPoly` per polygon) on Skiko, `android.graphics.Path` on Android, both
  kept by the painter between the strokes. The triangles stay for the meshes without outline.
* `StrokeAntialiasingTest` (JVM, native Ink): a slanted line drawn from its outline has more than
  a hundred pixels partly covered, more than ten times those of its triangles; a highlighter crossing
  itself is as light at the crossing as elsewhere.
* PencilNotes on the screen of 1x: the strokes at 100%, loaded or just drawn, have smooth edges
  where they were stair-stepped; the thumbnails of the library show a thin stroke whole where it
  broke into dashes.
* The screenshots of the repository draw no stroke: they do not change.

### Left for later

* **Less memory.** The outline is a second copy of the vertices (+57% of the geometry of the
  strokes in the probe). Two ways: the outline as indices into the positions of the mesh (a
  function more in the bridge), or the hit tests of the eraser and the lasso on the outline, so
  that the native strokes keep the outline only.
* **A cache of the paths** for what is drawn again at every frame (the selection being moved, the
  strokes of the others), as `androidx.ink` does, if building the paths shows in the frames.

## Decisions

Taken alone, revertible:

1. The outline is kept in memory with the mesh rather than computed when a tile needs it: a second
   modeling costs as much as the first.
2. No cache of the paths: a tile draws every stroke once, the path is built from one array per
   polygon (`addPoly` on Skiko).
3. Without the native engine the triangles stay (`drawVertices`): the outline of the triangles
   (`triangleOutline`) filled as one path would be antialiased too, but slower, for a case that
   happens only when the native library cannot be loaded.

## Risks

* Memory: the geometry of the strokes grows by 57% in the probe (the outline beside the
  positions and the indices of the mesh); the inputs, kept too, are not counted there.
* On Android a path is built with one call per vertex (`lineTo`): a tile of many strokes takes
  longer to rasterize there; to measure on a device.
